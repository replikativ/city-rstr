Who lives and works here?

This release covers Vancouver, UBC, the City and District of North Vancouver, and West Vancouver. It includes {{RESIDENTS}} residents and {{INCOMING}} attending workers who live outside the resident roster. Census counts constrain the population. Intact households drawn from census microdata preserve associations such as age, household composition and income. Several synthetic households can satisfy the same neighbourhood totals.

The atlas gives priority to resident and occupied-dwelling counts in each small census area. Within household sizes, it adjusts the weights of donor households against local age, tenure, labour and income margins, then samples whole households. Census rounding and differences in who each table covers can leave residents without a resolved household. Their person records remain in the population.

From a diary to a trip

The behavioural reference is an urban British Columbia weekday from the 2022 Canadian Time Use Survey. Drawing an intact diary keeps the sequence of commitments together, including the travel time between them. The diary clock runs from 04:00 through 04:00 the following morning. For adults, the assignment uses age and, where the donor pool supports it, worker or student status. Children use illustrative school-day or care schedules. Home and workplace assignments turn a schedule into proposed trips. Routing then tests which locations a traveller can reach within the time available.

The municipal transport studies show how diary weights can be adjusted against mode shares by home zone and income, and checked against a later survey. The regional work also tests an alternative workplace assignment using census commuting flows. These are separate fits with different geographies. The Downtown comparison below identifies the baseline used by the regional explorer and reports the sensitivity to workplace choice.

Opening a grocery on East Hastings

The market model takes eligible shopping occasions on foot and tests candidate grocers against street access and the surrounding diary commitments. The model assigns 40% of qualifying goods-shopping occasions to groceries, an assumed share. Each walking leg must fit its own diary interval at 80 metres per minute, and the two legs together have a 45-minute budget. Among feasible stores the shortest combined walk wins. The opening scenario adds a storefront and its trading hours. When a shopper switches to the new store, the baseline grocer loses that visit. A shopper whose walk becomes feasible for the first time adds an arrival without an identified incumbent loss.

Sales through time t = arrivals by t × assumed CAD per visit

At the default CAD 25 basket, an additional arrival contributes CAD 25. The diary and walking network determine the visit, while the basket sets its monetary value. Comparing predicted arrivals with store-entry counts would test demand. Transaction records would also allow the basket assumption to be tested.
