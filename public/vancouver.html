Grocery-opening evidence

Where shoppers and competitors come from

A qualifying diary records an in-person goods purchase at a store, with a walk on either side and a known next activity. The two scenarios keep the starting stop, next commitment and shopping duration fixed. People can defer a visit if every store is closed or too far away. The model covers individual walking occasions. Shared household purchases, other modes and spending need additional observations.

The candidate addresses were recorded as vacant in 2025. Opening hours, the grocery share and preference for the new shop are scenario assumptions.

Who the dots represent

Each dot is one synthetic city resident aged 15 or older. Rounded 2021 Census age counts for each dissemination area set the sample weights. Each person draws a whole weekday from the urban BC training diaries in the 2022 Time Use Survey. The replay shows people with at least one routed walk touching Mount Pleasant.

Where people walk

Homes start at census-area centroids. Recorded activities and walking times guide destination placement among August 2026 Overture places and nearby street nodes. Routes follow the shortest connected walkable OpenStreetMap path. Addresses, destinations and routes are inferred. The replay includes standalone walking legs between known stops. It omits transit access legs, walks with unresolved endpoints, children under 15 and residents outside city limits.

How the count works

Each comparison uses the four pedestrian approaches and the observation periods declared in the selected City workbook. Alberta and 12th has separate morning and afternoon counts. The model counts one passing per routed walk whose interior passes within 22 metres of the site, timed by distance along the path. Both endpoints must lie outside the buffer. Sample weights expand the model total. The home-neighbourhood filter changes the map only. The comparison uses the full simulated sample.
