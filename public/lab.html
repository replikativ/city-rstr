how this works
  1. 936,597 people are drawn from the census and the commuter statistics, each with a home cell, a workplace and a survey diary.
  2. Every shop trip picks a venue by measured floor area and distance; three demand classes (food, clothing, long-term goods) have their own kernels.
  3. Each visit spends a household's share of the district's purchasing power; leakage and in-commuter spend come from the published class balances.
  4. The kernel parameters are inferred from the 69 published district × class turnovers. The result is a set of plausible kernels, 48 short MCMC chains: an approximate posterior, not a calibrated one.
  5. A policy scenario re-runs the same people and money under a changed venue set, under every posterior draw. Its numbers are model results under stated assumptions, not forecasts.
