The Vancouver weekday model

At 17:00, the map can tell us how many residents have reached a hypothetical East Hastings grocer. To interpret that number, we need to know who was given a shopping occasion, which competing stores they could reach, and what purchase value the scenario assigns to each arrival.

1. Draw households within a census area

The unit of synthesis is a household. In each dissemination area, the atlas first reconciles the number of occupied dwellings and people, then assigns counts to household sizes. A five-or-more-person census bin can contain several sizes. The weighted size distribution of metropolitan Vancouver donor households supplies that split. Where census tables cover different populations, residents left outside the allocated private households retain their own person records.

Let h index a donor household of the required size, wₕ its census microdata weight, and xₕ its counts of members in the relevant age, labour and income groups, together with household tenure and gender composition. Within that size class, its probability is

P(h | area, size) = wₕ exp(θ · xₕ) / Σₖ wₖ exp(θ · xₖ).

The sum runs over donors of the same size. The atlas fits the coefficients θ against available area controls with a quadratic penalty to limit extreme weights. It then samples whole households to fill the required counts. This preserves the joint characteristics of each selected donor family. A match on neighbourhood income totals still leaves uncertainty about which individuals have each income.

2. Give each person a day

An adult draws an intact primary-activity diary from the urban BC weekday pool. The survey weight determines the relative probability within an eligible group. The assignment uses age and role when there is sufficient support and broadens the group when necessary. Younger children receive illustrative care or school schedules. An intact diary carries the sequence of activities and the minutes available between commitments.

The resident and external-worker cohorts answer different questions. An external worker contributes a commute and presence at work to the regional day. The East Hastings grocery experiment uses the resident diaries. Its result covers a narrower population than the count of people on the regional map.

The source schedules place trip boundaries on a five-minute grid. For the regional simulation, each resident and incoming worker receives one reproducible offset within ±2.5 minutes for the day. This models uncertainty within the reporting interval. It preserves the order and usual duration of trips. The resident diary stays anchored at 04:00. Street positions interpolate continuously, while the population tables record the resulting state at each whole minute.

The street layer uses a fixed resident sample and every 32nd attending incoming worker, showing journeys for which a supported street route was found. The marker limit controls how many active paths are drawn. The grocery calculation screens every synthetic resident’s diary and counts each eligible modeled visit once, independently of that limit. The 40% grocery draw represents a behavioral assumption about shopping purpose. There is no expansion from the visible dots to shop revenue.

3. Place people and count trips

Home and activity locations turn a diary into intervals. If zᵢ(t) denotes person i’s area or state at local minute t, presence in area a is

Nₐ(t) = Σᵢ 1[zᵢ(t) = a].

Travelling, outside and unresolved locations have their own states. The hourly flow into Downtown counts trips from another known area by their arrival times. The flow out uses departure times. A bridge counter instead observes vehicles at a particular crossing. Testing its prediction requires routing the relevant drivers across that location and matching the direction and hour.

The regional driver replay uses directed A* with estimated travel time as its cost. The posted limit, when available, or a road-class default supplies the speed. This run uses free-flow costs. A diary may allow more time than the route requires. Driving assumptions and coverage.

4. Check a grocery visit against two commitments

A candidate occasion must have an in-person goods-shopping stop between two walking legs. The model assigns 40% of eligible occasions to groceries using a reproducible draw. Let A be the preceding stop, B the following stop, and j a possible grocer. Let L₁ and L₂ be the diary allowances for the two walks. Each travel time is street-network distance, including endpoint access, divided by 80 metres per minute.

t(A,j) ≤ L₁  ·  t(j,B) ≤ L₂  ·  t(A,j) + t(j,B) ≤ 45 minutes.

The shopping stop must also fit inside trading hours. The baseline chooses the qualifying incumbent with the shortest combined walk. For example, with allowances of 10 and 15 minutes, a store requiring walks of 12 and 8 minutes fails the first constraint, even though the two walks together take just 20 minutes. A store requiring 8 and 11 minutes qualifies if it is open for the stop.

5. Compare the opening with the same day

The intervention adds the proposed store to the available choices and sets its opening hours. Holding shoppers, commitments and prior grocery draws fixed makes it possible to identify which individual visits switch. If a baseline visitor chooses the new shop, the incumbent records one lost visit. If the baseline has no feasible store, an arrival at the new shop has no corresponding incumbent loss in this model.

Δ salesⱼ(t) = basket × [opening visitsⱼ by t − baseline visitsⱼ by t].

Changing the basket changes the monetary result linearly. Changing the hours can change which shoppers can complete their visits. The following figures come from the selected regional run. Explore the hourly opening example.

With 08:00–20:00 trading, the shop receives 32 walking visits. 6 switch from incumbent grocers and 26 become feasible with the opening. At CAD 25 per visit that yields CAD 800 in modeled sales, including CAD 150 diverted from competitors.

6. What the data can test

Census controls constrain the roster. Counts by time and direction test the movement that follows from that roster. The workplace sensitivity run reassigns resident destinations using commuting flows while holding the external cohort fixed. Its bridge errors improve at some crossings and increase at others. Rail assignment has a separate coverage problem because the timetable pinned to the explorer omits SkyTrain lines. Regional travel evidence and comparison runs.

For retail, licences and storefronts establish where a shop could operate. An independent check would compare door entries and transactions with modeled visits and baskets over the same trading interval, accounting for customers who reach the shop by other modes. Until then, the result describes sales implied by a walking-access scenario. Source inventory for this weekday.
